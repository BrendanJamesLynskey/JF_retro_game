# 1. What makes it fun

These rules come before any hardware choice. When two options conflict, the one
that serves these rules wins.

## The rules

1. **Instant on.** Press power and you are playing in under a second. A handheld
   that takes 30 seconds to boot is one you stop reaching for. (This rule alone is
   most of the reason for choosing a microcontroller over a Linux Pi; see
   [Choosing the brain](02_choosing_the_brain.md).)
2. **Pick up, play, put down.** A round lasts one to three minutes. You can pause
   at any time and the console sleeps when you close it or turn it off. High scores
   are kept between sessions.
3. **It feels good in the hands.** Clicky buttons with a firm, short travel and a
   D-pad that rocks cleanly. Nothing rattles, and the weight sits in the middle.
   If the controls feel cheap, the games feel cheap.
4. **Juice.** Every action gets feedback: a sound, a flash, a little screen shake,
   a buzz of rumble. Small effects are cheap to add and make the biggest
   difference to how a game feels.
5. **Made by us, changed by us.** The games are our own, and the code is short and
   readable enough to change: a new level, a faster enemy, a different sound.
   Tunable numbers live in one place at the top of each game.
6. **Something to show off.** A two-player link cable, a high-score table with
   initials, a hidden cheat code, a custom boot jingle. These cost little to add
   and are what people remember.
7. **Tough enough to throw in a bag.** No loose wires, the screen sits behind a
   clear window, and the case survives a drop onto carpet.
8. **Retro on purpose.** Low resolution (160×120), a limited palette and
   square-wave sound. The limits are part of the charm, and they also keep the
   games small enough to finish making.

## What we are *not* building

- **An emulator box.** Running old commercial games is a different project with
  copyright problems, and it doesn't fit rule 5. The console plays games we write.
  (A Pi Zero 2 W variant could run PICO-8 or similar fantasy consoles; see
  [Choosing the brain](02_choosing_the_brain.md).)
- **A spec race.** A brighter screen or a faster chip only matters if it makes a
  game more fun.
- **Something that needs a laptop to start.** Once flashed, it stands alone.

## How we'll know it worked

- Someone who hasn't seen it before plays it for five minutes without being shown
  how.
- It comes out of a bag or pocket on a car journey without being asked for.
- Somebody asks "can I have another go?"
